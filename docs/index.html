How setups are found

Breakouts

A stock in the top 30% of its index by 1, 3 and 6-month performance, up 30% or more in its prior leg, then consolidating for 1 to 9 weeks without giving back more than 25%. The last five days must be tight, volume below its 50-day average, and price riding a rising 20-day average. The trigger is the high of that tight range. Buy the break of the opening-range high once price is through it, and place the stop at the day's low. The listed stop is the day's low capped at one ADR below the trigger.

Episodic pivots

A gap of 8% or more on at least 3 times average volume within the last five sessions, with price still above the gap-day low. Stocks that were flat or down before the gap are flagged as neglected, which Kullamägi treats as the stronger version. The trigger is the high since the gap and the stop is the gap-day low.

Parabolic shorts

Three or more straight up closes after either a 50% run in 20 days or a close five ADRs above the 10-day average. The trigger is a break of the latest day's low, the first crack, with the stop at the high of the run. Shorts are never taken while the stock is still making higher highs.

Terms on this page

ADR is the average daily range over 20 days, as a percentage. Risk is the distance from trigger to stop, also shown in ADRs. Away is how far the last close is from the trigger. RS is the stock's relative-strength percentile inside the chosen index, where 100 is the strongest.
